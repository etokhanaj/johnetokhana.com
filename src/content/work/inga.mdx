## Overview

Inga was social bookmarking: save anything from the web, keep it in collections you can edit, and share them. It was a side project I made in 2024 with a few friends, Abdulshaqur Suleiman among them, for ourselves first and for everyday heavy browser users who bookmark everything. <Led>I led design for Inga's browser extension, web app and monthly digest.</Led>

<Figure frame="plain" src="/assets/inga/extension.jpg" alt="Inga's browser extension, three screens: saving one link with a collection, type and tags; saving every open tab as a session, with some tabs unticked; and a saved session with Open all tabs" />

## What's the impact?

- Everything here went live: the extension, the web app, the monthly digest, sharing with roles, comments and reactions
- A few hundred people used it
- We never had a strong measure of how often people came back to what they saved, so this proved initial use, not long-term retention

## What's the problem?

Heavy browser users save a lot and come back to very little. A bookmark keeps a link and loses why it mattered: no note, no tags, nothing to bring it back. Open tabs pile up as a to-do list nobody finishes, and sharing a set of links means pasting them into a chat one by one.

## What did we ship?

### Save without leaving the page

The extension saves where you already are: one link with its collection, type, tags and an optional reminder, or every open tab as a session. Untick the tabs you don't need, choose whether to close them, and save. A session opens again with one click. The web app has the same save, with collections, tags and a reminder.

### Bring saves back

A reminder brings a save back when it's due. Its page shows the date, with its tags and the other collections it sits in.

<Figure src="/assets/inga/content-view.jpg" alt="A saved image's page: who saved it, the reminder date and time, its title and note, its tags and the other collections it belongs to, with Note, Comments and Share at the top" />

Once a month, a digest shows how much you saved, your most viewed collection, the content you came back to most, and a breakdown by type.

<Figure src="/assets/inga/digest.jpg" alt="The monthly digest: a greeting, the number of items saved that month, the most viewed collection, the content you interacted with most, and a breakdown of saves by category, with earlier months listed at the side" />

### Collections you can share

Collections are mood boards you can filter, sort and search, or switch to cards, a list or a table.

<Figure video="/assets/inga/webapp-interaction" alt="Inga's web app: one collection switched between mood board, card, list and table views; a saved image opened with its reminder, tags and other collections; the comments panel with a reaction picker; and Share Content, with a role for each person and a link to copy" />

Share one by email or username, with a role for each person: view, comment, edit or co-own, or with anyone who has the link. Comments sit on the saved item itself, beside its tags and the other collections it belongs to, with replies and reactions.

## Why it stopped

We started Inga for fun, and a product a few hundred people use needs steady hours from all of us. When we got busy, we didn't have them.

## Takeaways

1. A save only pays off when it comes back. Design the way back, the reminders and the digest, with the same care as the save.
2. Decide early, together, whether a project is for fun or for real, and agree the hours it needs. AI tools now carry much of a build, as they do on [Yada](https://yadabible.com), but they don't make that decision for you.
